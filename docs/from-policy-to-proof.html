From Policy to Proof

Closing the gap between “we have a policy” and “we can prove we follow it”

The Problem

Compliance teams define policies in documents. Engineering teams implement controls in systems. But proving that the systems actually satisfy the policies? That’s a manual, error-prone process of collecting evidence, mapping it to controls, and reformatting for auditors—often repeated from scratch each cycle.

1. Define

Gemara

Structure your compliance requirements into machine-readable building blocks:

  • Guidance — high-level requirements from standards bodies (e.g., CIS Controls, NIST 800-53)
  • Controls — testable conditions with configurable applicability for a specific technology (e.g., CIS Benchmarks). Where a condition is applicable, it must be true for satisfaction of the control objective
  • Risk — your org’s appetite, acceptance, and mitigation strategy
  • Policy — the scoped, time-bound rules tying it all together, including an adherence plan that defines the schedule and scope for evaluating assessment requirements and handling non-compliance notifications

GRC analog: turning your compliance framework into structured, reusable components instead of static documents.

2. Distribute

Policy Library

Publish policies to a central library that teams across your organization can pull from:

  • Versioned — every policy has a version; teams know exactly what they’re assessed against
  • Tamper-evident — cryptographic integrity ensures what you published is what gets pulled
  • Consistent — everyone scans against the same policy, eliminating “which version?” confusion

GRC analog: a controlled document repository, but machine-readable and automatically distributed.

3. Demonstrate

complyctl

Automatically prove compliance by scanning systems against the policies you defined:

  • Pull — fetch the latest policies from the library
  • Scan — specialized assessors evaluate each testable condition against applicable targets
  • Report — produce audit-ready evidence mapped to your framework

GRC analog: an automated assessor that runs your assessment plan and generates the evidence package.

Requirements → Shared Policies → Audit-Ready Evidence

What This Means For Your Program

✓ Policies are structured and reusable—write once, assess across frameworks
✓ Evidence is timestamped, traceable, and reproducible—not screenshots in a folder
✓ Assessments are automated and repeatable—continuous posture, not point-in-time
